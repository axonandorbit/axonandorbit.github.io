Webb found a star with no fusion inside

Watch on YouTube

JWST spotted MoM-BH*-1 at cosmic dawn, a few hundred million years after the Big Bang. It looks like a star but puts out ~100 billion times more energy than any star could. The team's best explanation is a black hole of ~100,000 solar masses wrapped in a dense gas cocoon about the size of our solar system: a 'black hole star', where the light comes from accretion, not fusion. It may also explain JWST's 'little red dots'. The authors note their picture is still evolving fast.
