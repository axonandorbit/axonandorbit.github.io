Scientists watched a single quantum of sound jump

Watch on YouTube

Quantum mechanics says vibration comes in discrete packets (phonons), but measuring an object's position can't reveal that. A Stanford-led team dispersively coupled a superconducting qubit to a lithium-niobate nanomechanical resonator (mechanical lifetime about 2.1 ms) and made repeated quantum non-demolition measurements of the phonon number. They heralded single-phonon states with 85% fidelity and observed quantum jumps between the first excited state and the ground state of a massive, vibrating object.
