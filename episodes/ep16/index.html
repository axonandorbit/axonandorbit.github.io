Something Is Stirring Betelgeuse From Inside

Watch on YouTube

Radio telescopes caught Betelgeuse changing shape in step with a suspected companion star. Using the VLA and ALMA at seven frequencies (22–485 GHz) from 2021 to 2026, a team led by MIT Haystack Observatory with the Center for Astrophysics | Harvard & Smithsonian mapped the red supergiant's extended atmosphere: gas near 3,500 K at about 2.7 stellar radii, cooling to about 2,220 K closer in. The star's radio ellipticity correlates with the orbital phase of the proposed companion Siwarha, which orbits at about 2.3 stellar radii — inside the star's radio-emitting atmosphere.

Caveats
The companion is not detected in these radio data; the link is indirect (a correlation between shape and orbital phase), and the authors say more observations across the orbit and more frequencies are needed. Optical images (Gemini 2025, VLT/SPHERE 2026) show a candidate companion, but its nature is still debated. Distances and radii for Betelgeuse remain uncertain.
