ChatGPT made them better at data science, until it was taken away

Watch on YouTube

In a randomized controlled trial, 487 management consultants were given ChatGPT access and training and asked to solve data-science tasks outside their skill set, tasks ChatGPT could not solve on its own. The treated group scored higher than the control group on all three tasks, though not as well as expert data scientists. Afterwards, without ChatGPT, they were no better at answering technical questions: the authors describe generative AI as an 'exoskeleton' whose gains depend on continued use, not as knowledge acquisition.
