A Robot Knee-Jerk Reflex With No Sensor or Chip

Watch on YouTube

Tohoku University engineers built a reflex for robot muscles out of air, a mechanical valve and a string. When an artificial muscle is stretched past a set length, a parallel "spindle" muscle pulls a string that opens the valve, and the muscle contracts. No electronic sensor or chip is involved. On a robot leg it reproduced the knee-jerk reflex: air began flowing about 10 ms after the tap and the knee kicked about 20°. With a separate phase controller switching the reflexes at each step, a two-legged robot walked on a treadmill (1.3 km/h) for one minute.

Licence: this video is released under CC BY-NC 4.0 (creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0). Credit: Axon & Orbit.

Caveats
A lab prototype. The walking robot still uses foot touch sensors, a microcontroller and solenoid valves to switch the reflexes by gait phase, and it needs a compressor and pressure regulators. It moved in one plane with its trunk locked, and an elastic rig carried part of its 3.2 kg weight. The valve is on/off, which causes some knee oscillation. Power and complexity savings are argued by the authors, not measured.
