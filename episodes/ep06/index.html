One mouse neuron, 70 cm of wiring: the brain's alertness system mapped

Watch on YouTube

Allen Institute researchers mapped the locus coeruleus, the tiny brainstem 'blue spot' that makes noradrenaline (alertness, attention, stress), in mice. They imaged nearly 35,000 of its neurons across the whole brain and fully reconstructed dozens of them: the average axon is about 35 cm long, and one reached 70.32 cm of total branched length inside a mouse brain about 1 cm long. Instead of broadcasting to the whole brain, the system is organised like a postal network: upper (dorsal) cells project mainly to the cortex and were active when mice changed their choices after a bad outcome; lower (ventral) cells project to the brainstem and spinal cord and were linked to whether the animals engaged at all.
