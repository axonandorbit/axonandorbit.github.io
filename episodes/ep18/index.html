A Neural Network That Computes With Light in a Fiber

Watch on YouTube

Researchers at the University of Tokyo and NTT built a neural network around a single loop of optical fiber. Light pulses circling the loop do the computing. Their new training method, state-skipping direct feedback alignment, learns from the final error and never needs to read the loop's hidden states. On the real optoelectronic setup it reached about 97% accuracy on handwritten digits (MNIST). In simulations of a modern network design it matched the standard version with about 40% less computation.

Caveats
The hardware test used a simple benchmark (handwritten digits). The 40% saving comes from simulation, not hardware. The authors note the current setup works serially and still trails GPUs in throughput and energy efficiency. Power figures (~1.1 W) are estimates. Large AI models were not tested, and energy or water savings for data centres are not measured.
