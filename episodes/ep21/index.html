Lab-Grown Mouse Muscle Makes This Tiny Robot Jump

Watch on YouTube

Researchers at ETH Zurich grew skeletal muscle from mouse cells on a soft, grooved hydrogel strip about 1.5 cm long. A computer simulation and an evolutionary algorithm designed the muscle and the scaffold together. The resulting strips curl far enough to jump off the floor of a lab dish: one single contraction lifted a strip 5.1 mm, a third of its body length. The team reports up to eleven times the range of motion of earlier designs with a similar amount of muscle, and built variants that swim, walk and grip.

Licence: this video is released under CC BY-NC 4.0 (creativecommons.org/licenses/by-nc/4.0). Credit: Axon & Orbit.

Caveats
The muscle is a mouse cell line (C2C12) grown in the lab. The robots work only in a dish of nutrient liquid and are driven by an external electric field; they carry no power or controller of their own. Performance varies a lot between strips: across five actuators the range of motion ran from 0.5 mm to 6.6 mm. The jump was measured in liquid. Bigger, longer-lived versions would need an artificial blood supply, which does not exist yet.
