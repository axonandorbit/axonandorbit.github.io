Could Webb's Densest Star Clusters Forge Black Holes?

Watch on YouTube

The James Webb Space Telescope, helped by gravitational lensing, has resolved compact young star clusters in galaxies seen less than a billion years after the Big Bang: the Cosmic Gems arc and the Firefly Sparkle. A Sapienza University of Rome team modelled what runaway star collisions could build inside such clusters. They estimate black-hole "seeds" of roughly 100 to 4,000 solar masses, with the most compact Cosmic Gems clusters reaching about 1,600–2,700. Seeds that heavy could help explain the giant black holes Webb finds so early.

Caveats
These are analytic and Fokker-Planck model estimates. No black hole was observed in these clusters. The authors put the uncertainty at a factor of a few or more. The clusters' rotation, binary fraction, internal structure and stellar mass function are unknown, and mass loss in stellar collisions is uncertain. The link to JWST's "little red dots" is only a suggestion.
