Scientists mapped the fat in the brain. Pregnancy rewrote it

Watch on YouTube

EPFL researchers built the first 3D atlas of the brain's lipids (in mice). A laser scanned 109 thin brain slices point by point, measuring 172 lipids in about 7 million readings. The brain split into 539 chemical territories, which the authors call 'lipizones'. Some match known regions; others give neurons and the distant areas their fibers reach the same chemical 'address'. In pregnant mice, the map shifted: the cortex was remodelled and galactosylceramide, a key myelin building block, rose across the white matter. Those changes were larger than typical male–female differences.
