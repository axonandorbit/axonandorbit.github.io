Fear makes your brain blur danger and safety

Watch on YouTube

McGill researchers put mice in an augmented-reality arena that could switch instantly between a 'danger' and a 'safety' context, while miniature microscopes recorded hundreds of neurons in the ventral hippocampus. Instead of sharpening, the danger and safety maps overlapped. The authors propose a trade-off: less precision, faster retrieval of threat memories ('better safe than sorry'), and suggest this could inform how we think about anxiety and PTSD. That link is a hypothesis; the study was done in mice.
