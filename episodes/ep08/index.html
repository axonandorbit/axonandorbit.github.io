The Solar System may fall apart much sooner than we thought

Watch on YouTube

Earlier models treated the Sun's future mass loss as smooth and found the planets' orbits stay stable far beyond the age of the universe. A Caltech-led study instead models it as random puffs of ejected mass, each giving the planets a small kick. In about 40% of their simulations the system was disrupted or violently scattered while the Sun was still a red giant, and their headline estimate is roughly a billion years of stability after the Sun becomes a white dwarf.

Caveats
