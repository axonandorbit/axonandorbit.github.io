How an Octopus Pours Itself Through a Tiny Hole

Watch on YouTube

How does a soft animal with eight arms get its whole body through a hole narrower than the gap between its eyes? Researchers filmed three common octopuses crossing a clear partition 30 times. Every pass followed the same order: probe with one arm, push 3–5 arms through, then anchor and pull. When the hole was tighter, the octopus added a fourth step, bringing the remaining arms through first and the mantle last.

Caveats
A small lab study, with 3 common octopuses for the main analysis and 4 Mayan octopuses for exploratory trials, in tanks with an acrylic wall. Each animal was tested at only one hole size, so the authors cannot fully separate the effect of hole size from individual preference. The soft-robot application is the authors' goal; no robot was built in this study.
